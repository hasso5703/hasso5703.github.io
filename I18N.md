# The French version of the site: rules

The site exists in English (the root, every URL as it has always been) and in French (under `/fr/`, same slugs).
English is the source: a French page says what its English page says, no more and no less. These rules are what
a translator, human or model, follows, and what `scripts/check-i18n.py` checks where a check can tell.

## Structure
- A French page lives at `src/pages/fr/<same path as the English page>`. Same slug, same sections in the same order.
- Layouts and components take `lang` (`'en'` by default, `'fr'`). Their own words live in `src/i18n/ui.ts`.
- Data (`src/data/lanes.ts`, `machines.ts`) keeps every number once. French text for a data entry overrides its English
  text fields only (`src/data/lanes.fr.ts`, `machines.fr.ts`), so a number cannot differ between the two languages.
- Guides are Markdown: `src/pages/fr/guides/<slug>.md`, with the same frontmatter keys as the English guide plus
  `lang: fr`, and `layout: ../../../layouts/Guide.astro`. `slug`, `published`, `rank` and every `href` of `sources`
  stay as they are. `title`, `description`, `h1`, `answer`, `faq`, the `label` of sources and related links, and
  `installNote` are translated. Internal links point to the French page (`/fr/guides/...`, `/fr/models/...`).

## What never changes
- Every number, date, version, model name, product name, file name, command, flag, environment variable and URL.
  Text inside backticks and code blocks is copied character for character, comments included.
- Names: Alzeo, DGX Spark, GB10, DGX OS, Qwen3.8, Nemotron 3.5 Bolt, Claude Code, opencode, SGLang, vLLM, llama.cpp,
  Ollama, Open WebUI, Hugging Face, GitHub, and every other product or company name.
- The meaning. Do not add a claim, soften one, or drop a caveat. A sentence that says what was not measured stays.

## French, as a careful technical writer would write it
- Natural French, not word for word. Short sentences. The reader is addressed as « vous ».
- Never an em dash or an en dash, anywhere. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or two sentences.
- French punctuation: a non-breaking space (U+00A0) before `:` `;` `!` `?` and inside « guillemets » (which replace
  English quotation marks in prose). No space before a comma or a full stop.
- Numbers in prose take the French form: a decimal comma (`65,2 tok/s`), a non-breaking space between thousands
  (`1 048 576 jetons`, `262 144`), and a non-breaking space before a unit or `%` (`99,2 %`, `128 Go`).
  Numbers inside code, commands, file names and model names stay as written (`Qwen3.8`, `v1.45.2`, `--port 30000`).
- Units: `GB` becomes `Go` and `GiB` becomes `Gio` (they are different units: never swap them), `KiB`/`MiB` become
  `Kio`/`Mio`, `tok/s` stays `tok/s`, `ms` and `s` stay.
- Titles and headings take a capital on the first word only, as French does.

## What the build checks (`scripts/check-i18n.py`)
- Every English page has its French page and the reverse (404 and share cards aside).
- Each page names its other language with `hreflang`, both ways, plus `x-default` on the English URL.
- For each pair, the numbers of the page's main content are the same once French formatting is undone.
